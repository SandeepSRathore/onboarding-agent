# Your First 90 Days

This is what we expect from you in your first three months, and what you can expect from us. Everyone learns at a different pace, so treat it as a guide, not a strict schedule. Your manager and onboarding buddy will adjust it with you.

## Week 1: get set up

Your only goal this week is to get comfortable and get your tools working:

- Sign in to Okta, Google Workspace and Slack, and set up MFA.
- Get GitHub access and connect to the VPN.
- Set up your development environment and **run flow-api and flow-web locally**.
- **Meet your onboarding buddy** (Arjun) and your manager (Priya).
- Complete your PeopleHub profile, including bank details for payroll.
- Read the *Engineering Workflow* doc.

It's completely normal if setup takes several days. Ask for help early.

## Weeks 2–4: your first 30 days

Now you start contributing code:

- **Ship your first PR** from a `good-first-issue` ticket. Pair with your buddy if you like.
- Read the *Architecture Overview* and *Coding Standards*.
- **Pick up your first sprint ticket** from your team's board in sprint planning.
- **Review your teammates' PRs.** Start with two, and ask questions freely.
- Join all team rituals (see below).

At the end of the first month you have your **30-day check-in** with your manager. To prepare, write a few notes:

- What you've shipped and learned
- What's been confusing or slow
- What support you'd like next

## Days 31–60: own a small feature

- **Own a small feature end to end**: from understanding the requirement, through writing a short **design note** in Confluence, to shipping it and checking it in production.
- Your design note doesn't need to be long. One page covering the problem, your approach and the alternatives you considered is enough. Get feedback from your buddy before you start coding.
- Keep reviewing PRs and start answering questions in your team channel when you know the answer.
- 60-day check-in with your manager.

## Days 61–90: go deeper

- **Shadow the on-call rotation for one week.** You'll watch how the on-call engineer handles alerts and incidents, without being responsible for them.
- Complete on-call training (this also unlocks production write access in AWS).
- Pick an area of the codebase to become the go-to person for over time.
- **90-day review** with your manager: celebrate what went well and set goals for the next quarter.

## Rituals

- **Daily standup at 10:30 IST**, 15 minutes. Share what you did yesterday, what you'll do today, and anything blocking you. Blockers are the most important part. Say them out loud!
- **Two-week sprints.** **Sprint planning is on Mondays** at the start of each sprint; the team chooses which tickets to commit to.
- **Retro every other Friday:** what went well, what didn't, and what to try next sprint.
- **Weekly 1:1 with your manager** on Mondays.

## How to ask for help

Asking for help is a skill, and we value it. Here's a simple rule:

1. **Try on your own for about 30 minutes.** Read the error message carefully, search the handbook and the codebase, and ask the Onboarding Buddy assistant.
2. **Then ask.** Post in your team channel or message your onboarding buddy. Include what you're trying to do, what you tried, and the exact error.
3. **Never stay stuck for half a day in silence.** A five-minute conversation often saves hours. Nobody will think less of you for asking.
